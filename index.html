2 · Reading20 minutes

Tourism in 2050: A Changing World

Adapted and simplified from Jenny Southan, “The future of travel: What might travel look like in 2050 (and beyond)?” (January 11, 2025). The text below is a classroom adaptation, not a quotation.

1. A new way to travel

Tourism will continue to change as technology develops. In 2050, travellers may use electric aircraft, autonomous vehicles and flying taxis. High-speed trains might replace some short flights. New transport systems could make journeys more convenient, but they will also need to reduce environmental damage.

2. Climate change and responsible tourism

Hotter summers may change when and where people go on holiday. Cooler destinations might attract more visitors. Hotels will need to use resources responsibly, and regenerative tourism could help restore damaged environments. Without careful planning, popular destinations would face greater pressure on their ecosystems and communities.

3. Smart travel and artificial intelligence

AI assistants will help many tourists organise trips, translate languages and find information. Smart hotels may adapt rooms to guests’ needs, while virtual reality might let people preview destinations. Yet technology will not necessarily replace the value of real encounters and local knowledge.

4. Health, inclusion and new destinations

Wellness tourism will probably keep growing as people look for healthy and meaningful experiences. Less-visited destinations may become more popular. Tourism businesses will also need to consider travellers with different ages and abilities. More accessible services could help more people enjoy travel.

5. What makes a journey valuable?

Technology will influence how we travel, but personal connections will remain important. Tourists might choose experiences that support local communities and protect nature. The future of travel will depend on decisions made today.

3 · Grammar in Context15 minutes

Predictions, Possibilities and Hypothetical Results

Read the examples from the text. What does each modal tell us about the speaker’s certainty?

FormMeaningExample
will + verbPrediction stated confidentlyTechnology will influence tourism.
may + verbPossibilityTravellers may use electric aircraft.
might + verbPossibility, often more tentativeCooler destinations might attract more visitors.
would + verbImagined or conditional consequenceWithout careful planning, destinations would face more pressure.

May and might often express similar degrees of possibility. Would does not simply mean a less certain future; it usually depends on an imagined condition.
